**How to read this file**
- Every figure has a source URL. **[V]** means verified on the source page this session. **[S]** means it came from a search-result summary of that page and could not be opened directly (paywall, 403 or PDF), so re-check it before it goes on a slide.
- **ESTIMATE** marks a number we derived. The reasoning is shown next to it. Estimates are never presented as sourced facts.
- Currency: US-sourced benchmarks are in **USD** and converted to CAD at **1.4196 USD/CAD**, the Bank of Canada daily rate for 2026-10-15 ([BoC](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/)) [V].

A few items are marked "not found" for that reason (cold-chain courier rate cards, butcher-shop margins, Ontario Soccer registrations, Instacart merchant fees).

**Ontario share: not found as a separate figure.** ESTIMATE: Ontario is about 38–39% of Canada's population (16.3M on July 1 2025 per [Ontario projections](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-population-projections) [V], against a national population of roughly 41–42M). Applying that share gives **Ontario ready meals ≈ CAD 1.9B** and **Ontario meal kits ≈ CAD 130M (retail-measured) to CAD 0.5–0.65B (incl. DTC)**. The **GTA (47.2% of Ontario)** would be about **CAD 0.9B of ready meals**.

**What this means for J&W.** The packaged ready-meal pool grows about 4% a year and is large. The weekly-kit model is stalling on price and churn, and Canada's DTC leader is in creditor protection. That leaves room for a **local, cooked, portioned, high-protein** offer that does not require cooking.
